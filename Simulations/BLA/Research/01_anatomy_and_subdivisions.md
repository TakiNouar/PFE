# 01 — Anatomy and Subdivisions

## Gross organization

The basolateral complex (often abbreviated BLA or BNC) comprises three main nuclei:

- **Lateral nucleus (LA)** — primary sensory entry point (especially auditory and somatosensory).
- **Basal nucleus (BA / BL)** — further subdivided into magnocellular, intermediate and parvocellular parts; strong reciprocal links with prefrontal cortex and hippocampus.
- **Basomedial nucleus (BM)** — less studied in fear paradigms; participates in broader emotional networks.

In rodents the LA sits dorsal to the BA. In primates the relative volumes and connectivity with sensory cortices are expanded.

## Cell density and counts

- Principal (glutamatergic) neurons: ~80–90 % of the neuronal population.
- GABAergic interneurons: ~10–20 % (species- and method-dependent; recent mouse whole-brain counts trend lower ~9 %).
- Feng et al. 2019 used a scaled model of ~27 000 cells for the rat BL nucleus, based on literature density estimates (~72 000 neurons in the full rat BL, scaled 1:2.7).

Local density is inhomogeneous; Feng placed cells randomly in a 1.4 mm cube while enforcing a minimum inter-soma distance >25 µm.

## Morphology of principal neurons

- Multipolar / pyramidal-like with 3–7 primary dendrites; one often more prominent (apical-like).
- Not arranged in parallel layers (unlike neocortex or hippocampus).
- Spiny dendrites; random orientation of dendritic trees.
- Typical soma diameter in models: ~25 µm; apical dendrite length hundreds of micrometres.

## Species notes relevant to modelling

Most quantitative electrophysiology and connectivity numbers used in computational models (Feng, Kim, Woodruff & Sah) come from **rat**. Mouse percentages of interneuron subtypes are similar but absolute densities differ. Human BLA is far larger (~11 million neurons estimated) and has reduced PV/CB colocalization relative to rodents.
