# 11 — Functional Roles

## Threat evaluation and associative learning

BLA assigns emotional significance to sensory events. LA is the primary site of CS–US convergence for auditory fear conditioning; BA integrates contextual and prefrontal information.

## Valence encoding

Positive- and negative-valence principal neuron populations coexist in the BLA:

- Genetically/spatially dissociable in mouse (Rspo2+ negative anterior; Ppp1r1b+ positive posterior); arrangement debated (intermingled vs partial segregation).
- Connected through **mutual feedforward inhibition via local interneurons**: one valence population recruits INs that suppress the other — competition / winner-take-more at circuit level.
- Finer mouse taxonomies report ~11 glutamatergic clusters, some mixed or social (Lim et al. 2024).

**Projection–valence correlation (Beyeler et al. 2018):**

- NAc-projecting → positive/appetitive (approach, reward).
- CeM-projecting → negative/aversive (fear, defensive); tend to suppress larger local ensembles.
- vHPC-projecting → contextual / mixed.

Projection identity and valence overlap but are not identical.

**Sources:** Kim et al. (2016); Beyeler et al. (2018); Lim et al. (2024).

## Expression versus suppression

- Output to CeA drives defensive/aggressive programs via brainstem (PAG) and hypothalamic routes.
- Prefrontal input (especially infralimbic) can suppress expression, largely by inhibiting CeA or recruiting inhibitory circuits within BLA.
- The blueprint’s “suppression gap” is intended primarily at PFC → CeA, with BLA supplying the raw evaluated threat signal.

**SOM as a suppression-state readout:** extinction engram data (Zhang et al. 2026) give a concrete interpretation of SOM activity:

- High SOM → fear circuit actively suppressed (extinction / controlled).
- Low SOM → fear circuit released, expression possible.

Suppression is not only PFC→CeA — it begins within BLA, where SOM level gates how much evaluated threat propagates to output. PFC may act partly by driving BLA SOM (two-stage chain).

## Extinction and safety

Extinction recruits distinct BLA ensembles and requires dopamine teaching signals. Safety and fear memories compete via local inhibitory circuits and differential projection patterns (see `08_plasticity_and_learning.md`).

## Emotional inertia (network level)

BLA itself does not sustain seconds-scale activity after a brief stimulus in isolation. Persistence arises from:

- neuromodulatory tone (especially dopamine),
- recurrent loops with CeA / PAG / hypothalamus,
- contextual reactivation from hippocampus,
- weakened prefrontal suppression under load,
- slower astrocyte-mediated anxiety tone (literature; out of scope for Run 1).
