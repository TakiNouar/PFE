# 08 — Plasticity and Learning

## Fear conditioning

- Classical view: coincident CS (tone) and US (shock) pathways converge on LA principal neurons; NMDA-dependent LTP at those synapses stores the association.
- Only a minority of LA PNs (~25 % in classic studies) become plastic and join the memory trace.
- Competitive synaptic interactions and intrinsic excitability differences determine which cells are recruited (Kim et al. modelling work).

## Interneuron plasticity

- Some interneurons express calcium-permeable AMPA receptors and support NMDA-independent LTP (Mahanty & Sah 1998).
- LTP at interneuron synapses can strengthen feed-forward or feed-back inhibition and thereby control the subsequent plasticity of principal cells.

## Extinction

- Extinction is not simple erasure; it involves new learning, often in distinct BLA ensembles (reward/extinction neurons vs fear neurons).
- VTA dopamine signals (including shock-omission signals) instruct extinction-related plasticity in specific BLA projection populations.

## Metaplasticity

- Prior activity can switch the sign of subsequent plasticity (e.g. LFS producing potentiation or depression depending on a priming HFS).

## Modelling implications

- Isolated BLA characterization does not require full long-term plasticity rules, but the substrate (NMDA, Ca-permeable AMPA on INs, neuromodulatory receptors) must be present if later multi-region learning experiments are planned.
- Short-term depression is already essential for realistic ongoing dynamics even without long-term plasticity.
