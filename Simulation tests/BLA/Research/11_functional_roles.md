# 11 — Functional Roles

## Threat evaluation and associative learning

BLA assigns emotional significance to sensory events. LA is the primary site of CS–US convergence for auditory fear conditioning; BA integrates contextual and prefrontal information.

## Valence encoding

Positive- and negative-valence ensembles coexist and are intermingled. Projection target (NAc vs CeA vs vHPC) partially predicts functional role, but valence and projection identity are not identical.

## Expression versus suppression

- Output to CeA drives defensive and aggressive behavioural programs via brainstem (PAG) and hypothalamic routes.
- Prefrontal input (especially infralimbic) can suppress expression, largely by inhibiting CeA or by recruiting inhibitory circuits within BLA.
- The “suppression gap” in the PFE design is intended to live primarily at the PFC → CeA step, with BLA supplying the raw evaluated threat signal.

## Extinction and safety

Extinction recruits distinct BLA ensembles and requires dopamine teaching signals. Safety and fear memories can compete via local inhibitory circuits and via differential projection patterns.

## Emotional inertia (network level)

BLA itself does not sustain seconds-scale activity after a brief stimulus in isolation. Persistence arises from:
- neuromodulatory tone (especially dopamine),
- recurrent loops with CeA / PAG / hypothalamus,
- contextual reactivation from hippocampus,
- weakened prefrontal suppression under load.

This is why isolated after-discharge was correctly unobtainable in the earlier simulation attempts.
